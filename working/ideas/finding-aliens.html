Finding Aliens using Unnatural Shapes

Summary

As objects get larger, the liklihood of them conforming to the "perfect" geometries of regular shapes or equilateral shapes seems low in my experience of the natural world - perhaps due to the exceptional chemical or biological conditions required for their formation at scale.

Could such structures in the universe be considered an indication of deliberate construction by artificial or intelligent forces and if so, would searching for them be a reasonable strategy for finding alien intelligent life?

At scale, regular shapes seem uncommon

In Nature, we find all sorts of shapes. We can start listing examples of common shapes in order of their number of sides:

  • The straight lines of a spider's web
  • The sphere of a planet (ish)
  • The oval of an egg
  • The spiral of a shell
  • The triangular deposits of Dogtooth Spar crystals (thanks to this siteand this one)
  • The cuboids of some crystals like Pyrite
  • The rounded hexagons of a beehive
  • The semicircles of a rainbow
  • The waveforms of a moving snake

However, how many truly straight lines have you seen much larger than a few centimeters?

  • A fissure in a rock like slate is rarely straight over more than a few centimeters
  • A spider's web quickly sags from weight and wind over more than a short stretch - particularly when not vertical
  • A crystal like the cubes in Fool's Gold rarely span more than a few centimeters
  • A flower's stem rarely reaches a great height before gravity and light affect its growth and stance

However, the moderately intelligent humans that populate this planet obsess over perfect edges and regularity because they're useful. An engineer wants a smooth piece of wood when designing the roof structure of a house, the artist wants their picture hung on a perfectly sqaure frame, the writer wants a book with pages that align... regularity is common across our artificial products both for beauty and practicality.

This extends into our spacecraft with straight metal pieces, rectangular solar panels, rounded fuel cells and pointed anntenae. There are no gentle spirals and disorganised nodules quite like in natural forms.

The larger the object, the less likely you will find a "perfect" shape

A crystal can't just grow in a straight line for ever because the exact chemical conditions requires may occur in small areas, but the larger the crystal gets the more the temperature might change, impurities might interfere or the concentrations of constituent salts would decrease. Furthermore, to keep every uniform would require constant, perfect agitation without bubbles or gradients created by turbulence. In Nature this seems like a tall order.

Life also doesn't generally achieve perfection at a large scale. Some crystal-like mineral deposits like those of shells are about as close as they get. Plants also do a good job in forming vascular networks with some regularity, their petals are arranged pretty perfectly in flowers but again these aren't ever that big. Some like a bamboo shoot might be the closest straight edge off the top of my head. Again, as with crystals this isn't surprising when you consider all the complexities of just "growing". There will be times of less food, of stronger winds, of drought, of infection, of some shrew trying to bit a bit off you or a rock crushing you... even oak trees left virtually alone in the middle of fields don't achieve a truly synmmetrical structure because winds and sunlight will fall on one side more than another.

Perfect simple geometries made of straight lines seem even less common. Can you think of a perfectly straight triangular formation in nature of the same size as a football or human? What about a square or pentagon?

Proposition: intelligent life creates unusually large regular geomtries

Skyscrapers, roads, pipes and railways are all built to regular shapes at large scales - deliberately for structural and functional reasons. These therefore could be used as an indicator of intelligent life elsewhere.

Counter-argument: it depends on resolution

It's true to say that planet Earth isn't perfectly spherical. However, looking at it from a distant position such as from the moon, it certainly does look very round. As we take a step back, irregularities disappear as resolution decreases. If we're trying to scan the universe for regular shapes, we need to prove it's regular to a specific order of magnitude, but our resolution likely won't be able to distinguish whether they're perfectly regular as they won't be in enough detail.

Getting around resolution

I think we'd need to have a couple of things before trying to search for intelligent life using this theory:

  1. A set limit for the ratio of regularity / scale i.e. at what scale does the regularity of a shape become statistically unlikely in the Natural World?
  2. Spectral methods that can can assess regularity with precision. For example, perhaps a long straight piece of metal induces and returns an electromagnetic wave when induced by an emitted wave? Or special fields can be observed in objects that conform to a specific type of shape? This is pure fantasy at this stage of course.

Conclusion

I'm relatively certain that truly regular shapes must get more unlikely with scale. However whether this is a good or useful indicator of intelligent life I'm not so sure! What do you think?
